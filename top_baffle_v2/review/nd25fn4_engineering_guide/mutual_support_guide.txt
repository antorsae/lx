¶ Using PLA Basic & PETG as Mutual Supports
Why this works: PLA and PETG don't bond well to each other — and that's exactly what makes them a great support pair. Supports snap off cleanly, leaving a much better surface finish.
¶ Overview
Let's look at the three examples below, using the incompatible filament techniques covered in this section.
In the image below, we can see 3 support approaches. The model was always printed in PETG, but the support filament varied between samples:
¶ Support Configurations Compared
¶ 1 — PETG model + PETG supports
Using PETG for both the model and the supports provides the shortest print time and the highest filament efficiency.
¶ 2 — PETG model + PLA supports
Only PLA is used for the supports. This results in the longest print time (5 h 3 min) and the highest filament usage. During printing, the toolhead needs to switch nozzles at almost every layer. Each nozzle change takes approximately 5 seconds, plus additional time to pressurize the nozzle at the purge tower.
¶ 3 — PETG model + PETG support base + PLA interface
The support base is printed with PETG, while the support interface is printed with PLA. This reduces the total print time by 1 hour and 20 minutes compared to the previous example. The time savings come mainly from fewer nozzle switches. The purge tower also uses significantly less filament — approximately half of the previous case.
¶ Surface Finish Results
Below, we can see the supported section. The image sequence is the same as above.
❌ PETG supports on PETG model
The bond between them is stronger. This makes the supports more difficult to remove and may leave marks or defects on the model surface.
✅ PLA supports on PETG model
The surface finish is improved. The supports are easier to remove, and the model surface remains clean and well-defined.
¶ ⚠️ Before You Start
This guide only applies to:
Bambu PLA Basic
Bambu PETG HF and Bambu PETG Basic
The following are not supported by this guide:
PLA Matte, PLA Silk, PLA-CF, or any third-party PLA
PETG Translucent, PETG-CF, or any third-party PETG
💧 Dry your filaments first. PETG runs at a high enough temperature that any moisture in PLA will cause stringing and oozing.
See → Filament Drying Recommendations
